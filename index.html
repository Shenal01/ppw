Introduction

Who I Am

A candid overview of my background, the work I do, what drives me, and where I stand today.

Hello, I'm Shenal.

I am Shenal Mario Peiris, a dedicated cybersecurity engineer currently completing my BSc (Hons) in Cyber Security at the Sri Lanka Institute of Information Technology (SLIIT), maintaining a 3.42 GPA. My work sits at the intersection of offensive and defensive security - conducting red team operations, mobile and web application penetration tests, SIEM engineering, and threat intelligence analysis.

I have been acknowledged in the WSO2 Bug Bounty Hall of Fame (2025) - visible under the "Asgardeo" section of the WSO2 Security Hall of Fame - and hold CVE-2025-60880, a responsibly disclosed vulnerability coordinated through the CVE programme and referenced on NVD/NIST.

As a Trainee Red Team Engineer at OREL IT (Pvt) Ltd. (Aug 2024 – Mar 2026), I have performed in-depth mobile application penetration testing on iOS and Android platforms including banking and Flutter-based apps, manually bypassed root detection and SSL pinning, conducted reverse engineering using Ghidra, JADX, APKTool, Frida, and Objection, and participated in full-scope red team engagements against healthcare and enterprise environments simulating real-world adversary TTPs.

In parallel, I operate as an independent freelance security researcher and cybersecurity consultant, assisting organisations in understanding and improving their security posture through risk assessments and penetration testing engagements. Prior professional experience includes work as a Data Entry Operator at Sarvodaya USA (2023) and as a Medical Laboratory Technician Apprentice at Medicheks Colombo (2021–2022), both of which sharpened my discipline, precision, and professional work ethic.

I am currently open to work and actively seeking a full-time Red Team Engineer, Penetration Tester, or Security Researcher role where I can apply my hands-on expertise to meaningful security challenges.

Reflective Journal

Learning in PPW

A structured first-person reflection on the Preparation for Professional World module (IT4070) — what I learned, how it challenged me, and how I apply it in my cybersecurity career.

01
What? — The Experience

Business Communication & Professional Writing

Before PPW, I treated communication as secondary to technical skill. LO3 changed that by showing me that in cybersecurity, clear reporting and stakeholder-focused language are as important as the findings themselves. I now write pentest reports with business impact in mind, not just technical detail.

LO3 — Business Communication Technical Report Writing Stakeholder Communication
02
What? — The Experience

Personal Grooming, Etiquette & First Impressions

LO1 and LO2 reminded me that professionalism starts before the technical discussion begins. Grooming, posture, eye contact, and tone all shape how clients judge competence and trustworthiness, so I now prepare for client-facing work with the same care I give technical delivery.

LO1 — Business Etiquette LO2 — Personal Grooming Professional Presence
03
So What? — The Significance

Interview Skills, Negotiation & Conflict Resolution

LO4 gave me a practical structure for interviews, especially the STAR method. It helped me present my CVE-2025-60880 disclosure more clearly and professionally. LO5 and LO6 also mattered because red team work depends on negotiation, rule-of-engagement discussions, and handling disagreements with clients calmly and professionally.

LO4 — Interview Skills LO5 — Conducting Meetings LO6 — Negotiation STAR Method
04
Now What? — Going Forward

Growth, Application & Continuous Development

PPW showed me that soft skills need the same deliberate practice as technical ones. I have already started applying the lessons through clearer LinkedIn and GitHub profiles, stronger executive summaries in reports, and better STAR-based interview answers. Going forward, I want to keep building confidence through public speaking and technical writing.

Public Speaking Technical Blogging Mentorship Continuous Growth
